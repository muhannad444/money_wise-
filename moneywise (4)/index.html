        Questions students actually ask

        How do I track what I spend?

        Add a category inside Needs, Wants or Savings, like Food with a plan of RM 200. Then tap + Add expense on it and enter each purchase, like Lunch RM 10. We subtract it for you: Plan − Spent = Left, and the bar updates instantly.

        What is "Grow your money"?

        A starting point for thinking about money you don't need right away. It shows the real cost, risk, time and skills behind a few ideas. None of them is guaranteed to earn anything, and it isn't financial advice.

        Is my money data safe?

        Yes. Everything you type — your income, your split, your items — stays in your browser's local storage. Nothing is sent to a server, and nobody but you can see it.

        What if my income changes every month?

        Choose Change amount or priorities at the top of your plan, enter the new total and tap Update my plan. Your budgets recalculate instantly, and categories you already added keep their own plans.

        Does this work with scholarships or a part-time job?

        Yes. It doesn't matter where the money comes from — allowance, scholarship disbursement, or a paycheck. Enter the amount you have for the month and the plan works the same way.

        Can I use different percentages than 50/30/20?

        Yes. Open "Set your priorities" in the calculator (or choose "Change amount or priorities" if you already have a plan) and tell us how important Needs, Wants and Savings are to you. The percentages adjust to match, with the reasoning shown.

        How does the Wants planner work?

        Tell us what something costs each time and how often you actually plan to do it — like Coffee at RM12, four times a month. We multiply it out for you, so a habit's real monthly cost is never a guess. Subscriptions like Netflix just use "1" since they bill monthly either way.

        Is this financial advice?

        No. MoneyWise is a simple planning tool based on a popular budgeting guideline, not licensed financial advice. For anything beyond everyday budgeting, it's worth speaking to a qualified advisor.
